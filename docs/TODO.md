## High priority

- [ ] **Scanlines at exactly 2 output pixels per line (240p content on a 480p output): none show today.** First user feedback (Reddit, 2026-11-27): a 15 kHz (240p) BVM-style look on a 31 kHz (480p) PC CRT monitor, done on a MiSTer with simple alternating black lines plus a contrast boost, and RetroArch's Interlacing shader (also in ReShade) as the filter they miss on PC. Measured the same day (`phoxium render`, a 320x240 image, mask off, flat tube, glow off, narrow spot of 0.1 mm): clean scanlines at 3 and 4 output pixels per line, **none at 2** (640x480). Cause (analysis, not yet proven in code): with an even number of rows per line the beam centre falls on the boundary between two rows, so both rows get equal light and the dark gap sits between two lit rows; no parameter shifts the raster by half a pixel (`source_center` off aligns the picture to the top-left, which still puts each line centre on a row boundary at 2 px per line). To do, in this order: (1) a **scanline phase** (a vertical raster offset of a fraction of a line or a host pixel, a physical parameter of the `beam`/`geometry` stages) so the lines land on pixel rows and alternate bright and dark at 2 px per line; prove it with a render test at 640x480 and a GPU-vs-oracle test; (2) decide whether to add a **simple alternate-line filter** as its own optional module (darken every other output row by a chosen amount, the RetroArch Interlacing shader's effect, plus a contrast gain), not physical but it is the "global image filter" they ask for; (3) check the whole-screen capture with a fullscreen click-through output at 640x480 (the way to use it as a global filter) and say honestly in the docs that it adds capture latency compared with a hardware pass-through. ## Performance

- [ ] **Bake the content-independent part of the chain once the parameters settle ("consolidate").** Idea (owner, 2026-11-27): after the look is tuned, the transformations that do not depend on the picture are constant, so precompute them instead of recomputing them per pixel and per frame. Decisions so far: **no parameter lock** (a lock makes tuning awkward and gains nothing): the baked data is a cache keyed on the parameters it depends on, rebuilt when one changes; while a slider is dragged the current analytic path runs, and after the values stay still for about half a second the baked path takes over; the output must equal the analytic path within the existing test tolerances (tests against the current path), so the switch is invisible; at most a status line ("optimised") in the menu. What can be baked: the geometry warp (curvature, convergence, overscan, rotation) as a per-channel offset map, the mask pattern as a map, the vignette, glass and bezel gain as one gain map, the gun law, white balance and colour matrix as one lookup table; and shaders specialised with SPIR-V specialisation constants (the interlace module already works this way, bit-exact when off); then fusing adjacent per-pixel passes (`scan`, `phosphor`, `encode`...), which are partly memory-bound (the phosphor history alone is 112 bytes a pixel). What cannot be baked: the beam (the sum over scanlines and amplifier samples, whose spot grows with brightness) depends on the content, and `screen` was 39 to 92 % of the GPU time in the 2026-11-19 measurements (curved tubes 4.7 to 6.1 times flat). Steps, stopping after the first if the numbers do not justify the rest: (1) measure the split of `screen` between beam, mask and geometry (GPU timestamps, `perf_profile`, curved and flat presets); (2) a cached geometry map and mask map behind equality tests; (3) specialisation constants; (4) pass fusion. Related, probably a bigger win for big captures than any baking: the resample-first fast path for sources with at least as many lines as the tube raster (`QUESTIONS.md`, "`screen` on big sources", dropped from the TODO on 2026-11-27).
