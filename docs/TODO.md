## Potential ShaderGlass gaps to fulfill

From the comparison of 2026-11-25 (`QUESTIONS.md`, "Comparison with ShaderGlass": its public pages only, nothing measured), sorted on 2026-11-26 by importance (first = most worth doing). Each entry is tagged **gap** (ShaderGlass does it, Phoxium does not), **check** (probably covered or a doubt to verify) or **decision** (the owner chooses a direction). The project's differentiator is the physical model, not the shader library, so the gaps are the owner's call. Removed on 2026-11-26 as not relevant: the dedicated-GPU and multi-monitor check (answered on two laptops, nothing misrecognised), the hardware and system reach gap (what is left, Windows 10 and AMD, is already under "Tests requiring other hardware"; a DirectX back end is not planned), and importing external shaders (by design, not planned; ShaderGlass is GPL-3.0, so no code or shaders are taken, inspiration only).

- [ ] **check: the mouse pointer stuck on the screen (their #299).** The one that can hurt a user: a pointer that stays hidden or shown wrongly, in a program that hides the host cursor over the output. Hand-check after F10, F11, Alt+Tab, a quit, a crash of the source, and in click-through (Phoxium hides the host cursor over the output while the menu is closed). About 10 minutes at the reference PC.
- [ ] **gap: motion clarity at high refresh (black frame insertion, a rolling beam scan).** The physical feature that fits the product best. ShaderGlass had black frame insertion in its alpha builds and moved it, with a CRT beam simulator, to a separate app (ShaderBeam). Phoxium models the afterglow (persistence) but not the scan: on a 240 or 300 Hz panel a rolling scan of the beam at the refresh rate would be physics, not an effect. First look at what a 300 Hz panel shows with the current persistence (the 300 Hz results in `QUESTIONS.md`; the 240 Hz mode was reported fine without a stats run), then decide.
- [ ] **gap, decision: an overlay without choosing a window (Desktop Glass).** The largest gap and the most work. A transparent window that shades whatever is behind it. Phoxium captures a window or a whole monitor and has click-through; the pieces of an overlay exist (the output window is already hidden from screen captures when a whole monitor is the source, the click-through option passes the mouse), what is missing is a region source: capture the screen area behind the output window, with the output excluded from its own capture. Decide whether it is a goal; if so, a design note first (the focus rules, the moving and resizing of the region, the cost of capturing the monitor).
- [ ] **check: the output scale below 100 % and a stretch (new in ShaderGlass 1.3.2).** Probably covered: `--aspect fill` or a forced ratio stretches the picture, `--scale-mode manual` with a fractional `source_pixel_size` and any output window size scale it down. Verify on the reference PC (a 640 x 480 source in a 300 x 225 window, a stretch to 16:9) and note the answer in `REFERENCE.md`.
- [ ] **gap: a video device from the command line.** Small and concrete. ShaderGlass takes `-input`; Phoxium has no flag to choose a capture card or a camera at start (only the picker). Check how the picker names devices, then add `--video <name>` (the matching rule of `--window`) so a launcher can start a capture-card session without the picker.

## Source colour targets

The chain stays as it is; a step at its head (`source_colour_target`, the per-game profile fields `colour_target` and `colour_variant`, the data files of `presets/targets/` with a provenance tag on every value) turns the colours of the source into what the device it came from would have shown. 25 targets are shipped (the list and what each does: `REFERENCE.md`; what the sources disagree on, the closed platforms and the research passes: `QUESTIONS.md`). What is left is below; the open points of each shipped target are in the notes of its data file and in the "Built" notes of `QUESTIONS.md`.

Across the targets:

- [ ] **A live look at the target details in the menu** (built 2026-11-26, checked only in headless previews `target/ui-preview/menu-colour-target*.png` and a headless click test): choose a target in the Source and placement panel and look at the label and variant line, the collapsed Notes and Provenance headers, the legend, the wrapping of the long notes at the menu width and at a large UI scale, and `phoxium --source-colour super_game_boy:2-a` (and a mistyped target and variant: the error lists what is accepted).
- [ ] **A live look on a real emulator window for every shipped target**, with the target on and off (stays for the owner). Covered headlessly on 2026-11-26 (QUESTIONS.md, "Headless look at every target"): the real `phoxium render` with the validation layer, for the 25 targets, variant 1 and one other, on and off, on a synthetic input made of each target's own reference colours, ramps, bars and non-palette colours (no validation message, nothing black, banded or NaN-like, pass-through pixels bit-identical). Not covered: a real emulator's output (its palette, scaling, filtering, the 8-bit quantisation of a real frame) and a live window.
- [ ] **Fix the order of a monochrome phosphor and a colour target (a verified bug, 2026-11-26)**: `decode.comp` takes the BT.601 luma of the source when `phosphor_type` is not P22, before the `target` slot, so every colour-dependent target sees three equal channels. Verified by render (QUESTIONS.md, "Headless look at every target"): with P31, `amstrad_cpc_green_screen` on is bit-identical to off (max difference 0 codes; red 58.5 against green 135.8 at the end of the ramps, the BT.601 order, not the 9 : 3 : 1 order of 143.9 against 37.0 that P22 gives), so the target recommended with a green phosphor does nothing; `commodore_64` on a colour picture equals the same target on the picture's luma (1.9 % of the pixels differ, from the luma's 8-bit rounding), red, blue, orange, brown and dark grey all land on the same dark grey. The notes of the CPC green screen ("the chain's decode stage takes the BT.601 luma of the grey") and of `super_nintendo_black_boost` ("acts on the grey the phosphor shows") describe an order the chain does not have for the first. Clean fix: decode keeps the collapse only while `source_colour_target` is none (bit for bit unchanged for every current user), and `target.comp` takes the BT.601 luma of its own output when `phosphor_type` is not P22 (the device's colour first, then the one-gun tube; the notes of the SNES table then change to say so); risk: the `target` slot gets a second job, the mono tests and the oracle (`phosphor_type.rs`, `crt_model::phosphor_types::mono_drive`) need a case with a target, and the SNES and Neo Geo curves act on colour before the luma instead of on the grey (a few codes). Alternative with no shader change: say in the notes and REFERENCE that a target is a no-op on a monochrome phosphor and drop the "use a green phosphor type" advice of the CPC green screen (it loses the target's purpose).
