## High priority

- [ ] **Scanlines at exactly 2 output pixels per line (240p content on a 480p output): none show today.** First user feedback (Reddit, 2026-11-27): a 15 kHz (240p) BVM-style look on a 31 kHz (480p) PC CRT monitor, done on a MiSTer with simple alternating black lines plus a contrast boost, and RetroArch's Interlacing shader (also in ReShade) as the filter they miss on PC. Measured the same day (`phoxium render`, a 320x240 image, mask off, flat tube, glow off, narrow spot of 0.1 mm): clean scanlines at 3 and 4 output pixels per line, **none at 2** (640x480). Cause (analysis, not yet proven in code): with an even number of rows per line the beam centre falls on the boundary between two rows, so both rows get equal light and the dark gap sits between two lit rows; no parameter shifts the raster by half a pixel (`source_center` off aligns the picture to the top-left, which still puts each line centre on a row boundary at 2 px per line). To do, in this order: (1) a **scanline phase** (a vertical raster offset of a fraction of a line or a host pixel, a physical parameter of the `beam`/`geometry` stages) so the lines land on pixel rows and alternate bright and dark at 2 px per line; prove it with a render test at 640x480 and a GPU-vs-oracle test; (2) decide whether to add a **simple alternate-line filter** as its own optional module (darken every other output row by a chosen amount, the RetroArch Interlacing shader's effect, plus a contrast gain), not physical but it is the "global image filter" they ask for; (3) check the whole-screen capture with a fullscreen click-through output at 640x480 (the way to use it as a global filter) and say honestly in the docs that it adds capture latency compared with a hardware pass-through.
