## [0.11.0] - 2026-11-26

### Added

- **The colour target's details in the Source section.** Under the **Colour variant** row, while a target is chosen, the menu shows the target's name and the variant in force, the one-line label of its data file, a collapsed **Notes** header (the file's notes) and a collapsed **Provenance (S, D, K, E counts)** header with a one-line legend of the four tags and, for every value of the file, its tag, key and source text. Everything is read from the data file (`src/ui/target_info.rs`, the layout is a pure function with unit tests); a headless menu test opens both headers and previews are written to `target/ui-preview` (`menu-colour-target*.png`). Not yet looked at live.
- **`--source-colour <target[:variant]>`** (also on `phoxium render`, after `--preset` and before `--set`): sets `source_colour_target` and `source_colour_variant` from the command line, over the settings and the saved source profile. The variant is a number from 1, or the id or name its data file gives (`super_game_boy:2-a`, `sega_mega_drive:regen`, `"Kega Fusion"`), case free, spaces and `-` read as `_`; a text of digits that is not a position is read as an id (`atari_8bit_gtia:800`); without a variant the first is selected. An unknown target or variant is an error at start listing what is accepted. See `REFERENCE.md`, Source adjustment.
- **ZX Spectrum 16K, 48K and Plus PAL composite colours** (`source_colour_target = sinclair_zx_spectrum_48k_pal`, `source_colour_variant` 1 or 2: **1** a fixed chroma gain at the standard PAL weights over the normal white (the default), **2** a television whose chroma gain follows the burst; both rest on the probe-test levels): a Spectrum emulator picture (the usual 3-bit colours, the normal level 192, 205 or 216, the bright 255) recognised colour by colour (29 reference colours, 15 colours, bright black is black) and shown as the colour a PAL decoder drives the tube with, the `signal` kind (no shared code change). **Derived and relative only:** the levels are the output voltages of the probe-test circuits of the Ferranti 5C102 ULA specification (read by eye from enlarged scans, quoted with attribution; the Amstrad patent figures draw the same networks), with a test load and not a Spectrum's own load resistors, LM1889 and modulator: the luma drops 0.30, 0.61 and 0.15 V add (normal white 1.06 V against 1.40 V for the bright white, a ratio of 0.757), the U and V drops are B-Y and R-Y with gains of 1.10 and 2.0 and give the colour angles within 0.7 degree of the PAL colour bars, the burst is 1.294 V at 133.4 degrees against the standard 135. The burst is large against the colours, so variant 2 (the burst-following television) shows a desaturated picture and variant 1 (the default) normal colours near 190 to 199, close to the emulators' 192 and 205; neither is a measurement. Not distinguished: the issues (the 6C001 is assumed to match), the loads, the clamp circuit. Recommended composite module settings: PAL, delay line, pixel clock 7.0 MHz. The 128K, +2, +2A and +3 are the RGB target below.
- **ZX Spectrum 128K, +2, +2A and +3 RGB colours** (`source_colour_target = sinclair_zx_spectrum_rgb`, `source_colour_variant` 1 to 6: the emulator's normal level times the ratio of the analogue levels; **1** 205 (0xCD) with 0.68 (the default), **2** 205 with 0.63, **3** and **4** 192 (FUSE's) with 0.68 and 0.63, **5** and **6** 216 (the 85 percent level) with 0.68 and 0.63): a Spectrum emulator picture (every channel 0, a normal level or 255) with the normal level moved to its share of bright at the monitor, 173 of 255 (0.68, the +2A and +3 schematic computation through a 330 ohm cable) or 161 (0.63, one author's 128K SCART cable calculations), the `dac` kind with three points per channel (no shared code change). **A documented choice, load-dependent, not a measurement**: no measurement of a real Spectrum exists in the sources, and the emulators' normal levels are conventions. The 48K's 0.85 (a clone-design blog, composite) and the Harlequin readings (0.67 to 0.74) are notes, not variants; the 48K PAL composite encode and the 128K's TEA2000 composite are not built (no per-colour Y, U, V levels in anything readable). Unit tests (the 27 level combinations, no identity variant, the validation), GPU-vs-oracle cases on the six variants.
- **Apple II, II+ and IIe lo-res colours** (`source_colour_target = apple_ii_lores`, `source_colour_variant` 1 to 4: **1** the ideal waves (the dark and light colours' chroma 0.707 of the mid colours') on a television whose chroma gain follows the burst (the default), **2** Munafo's ratio 0.60 with the same gain, **3** and **4** the same two ratios with a fixed chroma gain): an Apple II emulator picture in lo-res (MAME's palette) shown in the 16 colours an NTSC television decodes from the machine's composite signal, so the tube's own gamma, primaries and white take over. **Derived from the circuit logic, not measured colour by colour (no vectorscope or colorimeter reading of any Apple II colour was found); only the level set is a measurement**: Gayler, 'The Apple II Circuit Description' (Sams; numbers only), open circuit with the level pot at maximum, sync 0 V, black 0.5 V, white 2.0 V, burst 0.7 V peak to peak (so a span of 1.5 V, the burst 0.2333 of it). A lo-res colour is a four-bit pattern on the 14.318 MHz clock, a rectangular wave at the colour frequency: the luma is the lit fraction (0.25, 0.5, 0.75) of the span, the phase the sum of the phasors of the lit bits on the 45 degree grid with the burst at 180 degrees (Munafo's table, with Gayler's phases of violet, blue and green as a check), the amplitude the fundamental of the wave (0.6366 of the span for two lit bits, 0.4502 for one or three, a ratio of 0.707 as Ticmanis derives it; Munafo's table has 0.60; a decoder that follows the burst brings it to 20 IRE, a factor 0.857, a fixed-gain one keeps the span ratio). The 0.75 V black of the Reference Manual and the IIe Technical Reference is a note, not a variant (the burst at that pin is not documented). The II+ resistors (R6 2.7k burst, R7 1.5k video, R8 2k sync, R10 27 ohm, R11 200 ohm pot; baldengineer/bit-preserve, CC BY 4.0) agree with Gayler's levels and are in the notes. **Recognition uses MAME's own lo-res palette as the reference** (16 RGB entries via `rgb_references`, BSD-3 notice in `docs/THIRD-PARTY.md`; the AppleWin palette is GPL and not used): the model's colours on variants 1 and 2 are 0.025 from it on average and 0.05 at most, so the default changes a MAME picture only a little. Shared code extended: a revision of a `signal` file may give `amplitudes`, one chroma amplitude per colour (colours that do not share one amplitude). Not modelled: hi-res and double hi-res artifact colours (a bit-pattern decode an emulator picture no longer carries), text-mode fringing, the IIgs (an identity), monitors, the 25 ns flip-flop delay, the hue trimmer and the level pot of each unit. Unit tests recompute the 16 colours from the bit phasors, the GPU stage is checked against the oracle on every variant.
- **Virtual Boy colours** (`source_colour_target = nintendo_virtual_boy`, `source_colour_variant` 1 or 2 for the emulator's picture: **1** a grey picture, Mednafen's default tint 0xF0F0F0 (the default), **2** a red picture, a red tint or palette): the console's four levels as the light of its red LEDs on black, the `matrix` kind (no shared code change). The light of an LED is proportional to its on-time (a design statement of Nintendo's manual, second-hand, and of the patent US5682171A, not a measurement), and the three red shades are BRTA, BRTB and BRTA + BRTB + BRTC (commercial games typically 0, A, 2A, 3A, second-hand), so the four levels are evenly spaced in light, 0, 1/3, 2/3, 1. The target undoes the emulator's 1 / 2.2 power encode (Mednafen and Beetle VB, numbers only), keeps red only (green and blue rows are zero, black stays black) and encodes the red light again for the chain with 2.2, like the other colour targets; the grey of variant 1 is the BT.601 luma of the linearised channels. **On the red channel the two exponents cancel**, so variant 2 changes nothing on a picture that is exactly red (it only drops a green or blue part of a tint); the effect of the target is to turn a grey picture into red light. **The colour is the sibling product's:** the only sourced colour is the Private Eye P4 datasheet (Reflection Technology, 1991, an unofficial archive copy with no licence: 660 +/- 20 nm red, 2 foot-lamberts and 70:1 contrast, quoted as context); the Virtual Boy has no published luminance, chromaticity or measured on-time-to-light curve, 660 nm is beyond the sRGB gamut so (1, 0, 0) is a stand-in (the colour on the screen is the tube's red phosphor), and no black lift is modelled. Mednafen's `vb.ledonscale` (1.75) is the emulator's own brightness coefficient and is not undone (it may clip); Red Viper's (v / 255 x 1.75)^0.9 table is not the 2.2 encode and is only approximately covered; Beetle VB's 'black and red' option was not checked. Unit tests (the four levels at 0 : 1 : 2 : 3 of light, hand derivation, no green or blue, the validation), a shader push test and GPU cases against the oracle (max 2.4e-4).
- **Game Boy, Pocket and Light colours** (`source_colour_target = game_boy_lcd`, `source_colour_variant` 1 to 5: **1** BGB 'reality' DMG (the default), **2** BGB 'reality' Pocket, **3** SameBoy DMG, **4** SameBoy Pocket, **5** SameBoy Light): a grey Game Boy emulator picture (its grayscale palette) shown in the four colours of a real screen, the `palette` kind (the luma picks a position between four colours, lightest first; a grey source is required). **Photographs, not measurements, and no instrument measurement of these screens was found anywhere:** variants 1 and 2 are the 'reality' colours of the BGB emulator (photographs under colour-accurate light, ColorChecker-corrected to sRGB gamma 2.2; the numbers read from the palette chunk of its swatch images; the slot order, LCD off first then shade 0 to 3, is inferred and not proven). **BGB is closed-source freeware with no licence stated: the numbers are used with attribution, its author has not been asked** (TODO). Variants 3 to 5 are SameBoy's palettes (`Core/display.c`, MIT / Expat, Copyright (c) 2015-2026 Lior Halphon, notice in `THIRD-PARTY.md`; 'not faithful' for the DMG per its author). The Gambatte and other GPL palettes are not used. The LCD-off colour is not shown (the palette kind has no background colour: the four shades are used), and the contrast wheel and the Light's backlight spectrum are not modelled. **Shared code:** the `palette` kind now accepts `colour_bits = 8` with 0xRRGGBB words (the Super Game Boy keeps `colour_bits = 5`). Unit tests, GPU cases against the oracle for four variants, docs.
- **MSX2+ / turbo R (V9958) palette colours** (`source_colour_target = msx2_v9958`, `source_colour_variant` 1 or 2 for the emulator's expansion of the 3-bit palette: **1** bit repetition, 0, 36, 73, 109, 146, 182, 219, 255 (the default), **2** blueMSX's truncated 255 n / 7): the eight levels of each palette channel shown as the V9958's 5-bit DAC gives them, the code floor(4.5 n) = 0, 4, 9, 13, 18, 22, 27, 31 at code x 255 / 31 = 0, 33, 74, 107, 148, 181, 222, 255 (a `dac` of eight points, the same on the three channels). **One unsourced mapping** (Grauw's 'The YJK screen modes': no derivation; the table equals round(31 n / 7), a plain 5-bit quantisation; openMSX builds its V9958 palette through the same expansion after a comparison with a real turbo R, as relayed from a text summary of its source) **on a DAC the V9958 booklet labels linear (the analogue levels were read afterwards, see Changed); the effect is at most 3 of 255 (1.4 percent)**. openMSX on a V9958 machine already shows these levels, so the target is not for it (an identity, no variant); the V9938 has no documented mapping (identity, no target); the YJK and YAE decode is not built (an emulator has already decoded those pixels to RGB). No shared code change.
- **Amstrad CPC colours and green monitor** (`source_colour_target = amstrad_cpc`, `source_colour_variant` 1 to 4 for the half level the emulator shows: **1** 128 (the default), **2** 127, **3** MAME's 96, **4** 128 with a per-channel half level (red 96, green 108, blue 92 of 255) computed from the output resistors of the CPC6128 service manual (680 ohm pull-ups, 270 ohm series, 3.3k, 1k and 10k luminance resistors, the CTM644's 100 ohm input; ideal pin drivers; green is 4 to 7 points above red and blue, the absolute level is uncertain); and `amstrad_cpc_green_screen`, variants 1 to 4): the first moves the Gate Array's middle colour level (an emulator shows it as about 128) to **0.40 of full scale, 102 of 255**, on the three channels (a `dac` of three points, black and full kept), the CPCWiki Gate Array page's figure credited to Grim and confirmed by nocash and the CPC Plus ASIC's 6 / 15; **second-hand, the CPCWiki site and the Grimware scope pages could not be read, and it conflicts with the wiki table's 0.5 and with nocash's loaded scope readings (a ratio of 0.5)**. Variants 1 to 3 keep one half level for the three channels, no monitor gamma, no modulator. The second is the GT64 and GT65 green monitor's 9 : 3 : 1 sum of the three outputs, (9 R + 3 G + B) / 13 on all channels (the `matrix` kind, equal rows; use a green phosphor type), as 27 linear steps (variant 1) or with the half level at 0.40 (variants 2 and 4, an exponent that puts the emulator's half level on 0.40, an estimate; with 0.40 the steps are uneven and two colours share a grey). No shared code change. Unit tests, GPU cases, docs.
- **Atari 2600 colours** (`source_colour_target = atari_2600_tia`, `source_colour_variant` 1 to 6: **1** a hue delay of 25.7 degrees with a chroma of 0.343 (the default), **2** 24 degrees, **3** 26.7 degrees, **4** 25.7 degrees with a chroma of 0.30, **5** 25.7 degrees with a chroma of 0.23, **6** 25.7 degrees with the luma ladder of the 2600A from revision 14): an NTSC Atari 2600 (VCS) picture shown in the 128 colours (hue x 8 + luma, the colour register divided by 2) an NTSC television decodes from the TIA's composite signal, so the tube's own gamma, primaries and white take over. **Unverified: derived from a mailing-list analysis and emulator notes, no hardware measurement found** (Eric Ball, Stella mailing list, December 2003, schematic-computed and self-described as 'still off', read second hand: luma 0.045 x (COLU and 15) of a 92.5 IRE span, so 0.09 a step and 0.63 at the top, linear; chroma 20 IRE over 92.5 = 0.2162, which over the luma range is 0.2162 / 0.63 = 0.3432; hue = 180 - (hue - 1) x 24; the TIA manual: hue 1 in phase with the burst; Gopher2600's notes: 25.7 to 26.7 degrees a step, saturation 0.3 assumed; jigo2600: 14 stages of 24 degrees; MAME's notes: about 23 degrees at power-on to about 28 after a long run, 26.2 active; no code used). The hue step is the pot of the real board (variants 1 to 3), the chroma level has three published readings (variants 1, 4, 5: Ball's, Gopher2600's assumed 0.30, the magnitude of MAME's hue 1 chroma 0.2303). The burst phase is a recorded conflict, not resolved (hue 1 on the burst here, as the TIA manual has it; the Gopher2600 notes turn it by 57 or 32.72 degrees, MAME's hue 1 is 'gold' about 23 degrees from it; the tube's tint moves it). **Recognition uses MAME's own TIA NTSC palette as the reference** (128 RGB values computed once from the numbers of `src/mame/atari/tia.cpp`, BSD-3-Clause, copyright-holders Wilbert Pol and Stefan Jokisch, BSD-3 notice in `docs/THIRD-PARTY.md`; a unit test recomputes them from the typed (I, Q) table), within 0.05 in drive voltage: MAME's palette is recognised exactly; any other emulator palette (Stella's is GPL and was not read) was not checked. The model's colours are 0.19 on average and up to 0.37 from MAME's palette at the default (0.11 for the chroma of 0.23, 0.24 for a step of 24; a model reference would have taken 66 of MAME's 128 colours for another colour and 45 for none). **What the target shows is the model's colour, not MAME's.** The luma network was then read from the schematics of the Atari 2600/2600A Domestic Field Service Manual (LM2, LM1, LM0 and SYN through 12k, 24k, 47k and 10k behind buffers on the CX2600, 27k, 56k, 110k and 27k on the early 2600A, 27k, 47k, 110k and 24k from revision 14): the CX2600 and the early 2600A are linear within 0.3 % and 1 % of the span, so there is no variant for them and the default stays linear, and the 2600A from revision 14 departs by up to 3.8 %, which is **variant 6** (25.7 degrees, chroma 0.343, the ladder luma, recomputed from the resistors by a unit test and checked on the GPU). **PAL (only a fitted 30-degree model exists) and SECAM (an 8-colour table) are not built.** No shared code change; unit tests, a GPU case, docs.
- **NEC PC Engine / TurboGrafx-16 / SuperGrafx composite colours** (`source_colour_target = nec_pc_engine_composite`, `source_colour_variant` 1 or 2: **1** Kitrinx's fitted decode with a V gain of 0.96 (the default), **2** the same with a V gain of 14/15): a PC Engine picture shown in the colours of the console's composite output. The HuC6260 colour encoder does not encode the RGB it is given: it looks up each of its 512 colour codes (3 bits each of green, red and blue) in a silicon table that gives a 5-bit luma Y and 5-bit colour-difference codes U and V; furrtek read that table from a real console and it is shipped as data (512 entries of [U, V, Y], parsed from `bfbii.h` of Kitrinx's TG16_Palette repository: ranges 1 to 30 for U and V, 0 to 31 for Y, black {16, 15, 0}, white {16, 15, 31}, the seven steps of blue {18, 15, 0} to {30, 15, 3}, the first red {14, 18, 1}). The decode is Kitrinx's, fitted to vectorscope plots of one PC Engine Duo-R: Y over 31, U and V as 1's complement codes over 15 times the BT.601 maxima 0.436 and 0.615 (times 0.96 for V), the chroma plane turned by -10.4 degrees, the exact BT.601 matrix, a desaturation toward the luma that keeps 192/255, no gamma. It was written independently and reproduces the converter's published palette byte for byte on all 512 entries. **The numbers of the fit are one person's estimate on one console; the phase varies from console to console.** An emulator's picture is a function of the 9-bit code, so the code is recovered **exactly** from the three 3-bit levels (255 n / 7 rounded or 36 n are both accepted) and replaced by the colour of its entry, with no nearest-colour search; any other pixel (a filtered or scaled picture, another level rule) passes through unchanged. Use it with an emulator that shows the plain RGB colours; leave it off for one whose palette already is the composite one. **Licence, said plainly:** the table is NEC's silicon data; the file in the repository has no licence header and GitHub reports none (the converter in the same repository is MIT), so the numbers ship as hardware facts with attribution (the data file and `docs/THIRD-PARTY.md`); no code was copied and nothing was taken from the MiSTer core's (GPL) palette. Not built: an RGB output (the chip's raw RGB has a large DC offset; no measured level or gamma exists), the analogue levels and the burst, the SuperGrafx and later chip revisions' differences (none recorded). Shared code extended: a new `lut` kind (`Lut`, `lut_index`, `ones_complement`, `decode_pbpr` in `crt-model`; the shader's kind 6; the 512 colours travel in the frame block's existing 512-entry target table, so the uniform block stays at 8288 bytes); every other target is unchanged. Oracle tests, unit tests of the data and the validation, a GPU test of all 512 codes in both expansions against the oracle.
- **Atari 7800 colours** (`source_colour_target = atari_7800_maria`, `source_colour_variant` 1 to 5: **1** a hue delay of 25.7 degrees with a linear luma (the default), **2** 26.7 degrees, **3** 24.7 (cold console), **4** 27.7 (warm console), **5** 25.7 degrees with the luma of the 10k, 20k, 39k, 82k ladder): an NTSC Atari 7800 picture shown in the 256 colours (hue x 16 + luma of the colour register) an NTSC television decodes from the MARIA's composite signal, so the tube's own gamma, primaries and white take over. **Unverified: computed from an anonymous wiki page and an emulator author's notes, with no hardware measurement found** (the Atari 7800 Programming wiki, schematic-computed: luma resistors SYNC 8.2k, LUM3 10k, LUM2 20k, LUM1 39k, LUM0 82k ohm, Y = (COLU and 15) x 0.0526, saturation 40 IRE / 185 IRE = 0.2162, hue = 180 - (hue - 1) x 25.714, burst 180; the 7800 development wiki's two historic pot calibrations 25.7 and 26.7 and a drift of 24.7 cold to 27.7 warm; MAME `a7800.cpp`, BSD-3-Clause, numbers with attribution: 25.7, hue 1 and 15 near-identical at 25.7, hues 14 and 15 on 1 and 2 at 27.7; no code used). The amplitude is the saturation over the luma of COLU 15 on the wiki's scale (0.2740; 0.2734 for the ladder luma, which the unit test recomputes from the resistors and which differs from the linear luma by at most 0.0032 of the range). **Recognition uses MAME's own 25.7 degree palette as the reference** (256 RGB entries, BSD-3 notice in `docs/THIRD-PARTY.md`), not the model as the Atari 8-bit target does: the model's colours are 0.12 on average and up to 0.21 from MAME's palette (MAME's hue 1 is 'gold', the model's is on the burst, about 23 degrees apart) and as a reference it recognised only 57 of its 256 colours; no other emulator palette was checked. PAL is not built (the wiki gives a contrast, sync and burst but no PAL hue delay). Shared code unchanged (the grid and `rgb_references` of the Atari 8-bit and CGA targets); unit tests (hand-derived colours, the wiki's contrast and the ladder recomputed, the delay geometry, MAME's palette recognised exactly and the model reference refused, validation), GPU case.
- **Mega Drive shadow and highlight levels** (`sega_mega_drive`, variant 1 Genesis Plus GX): the target's map now has all 15 measured levels of the divider (0, 29, 52, 70, 87, 101, 116, 130, 144, 158, 172, 187, 206, 228, 255 of 255, TmEE's 15 millivolt readings scaled so that 951 mV is 255; the normal mode is the even levels as before, shadow the first eight, highlight the last eight). Genesis Plus GX shows each level as 17 times its index (normal 34 n, shadow 17 n, highlight 119 + 17 n), so the three modes sit on the same 15 points with no ambiguity and a shadow or highlight pixel comes out at its measured level (29 for the first shadow step, where the 8-point line gave 26); a normal picture is unchanged. Kega Fusion and Regen keep the 8 normal levels (their shadow and highlight half steps are not documented in the repository or on a page read). Shared code extended: a `dac` target holds up to 15 points (`Dac` arrays of 15, `Dac::len`; a variant's own `output` sets its number of codes; a green or blue of its own stays at 8 codes at most); the shader has a second dac layout, kind 5 (15 inputs and 15 outputs in the same 32-byte push table, the push block stays at 100 bytes); kind 3 and every other target are unchanged. Still open: the white scale (255 against 238), the black level, the loading of the encoder.
- **IBM CGA composite colours** (`source_colour_target = ibm_cga_composite`, `source_colour_variant` 1 to 4: **1** the old card with a fixed chroma gain (the default), **2** the new card with a fixed chroma gain, **3** and **4** the old and the new card on a television whose chroma gain follows the burst): the 16 CGA colours of a picture shown as an NTSC monitor or television decodes the card's composite output (the modes with the colour burst on), in place of the canonical RGBI palette an emulator draws. The card makes six square waves of the colour frequency at fixed phases (reenigne, 2006: black 00000000, blue 00001111, green 01111000, cyan 00111100, red 11000011, magenta 10000111, yellow 11110000 which is also the burst, white 11111111), one wave per colour bits, none for the intensity bit; they are one wave turned by whole 45 degree steps, so every chromatic colour has the same fundamental and the phases are blue 0, magenta 45, red 90, yellow 180 (the burst), green 225 and cyan 270 degrees (derived: the direction of the turn is not in the source and is fixed by the hues the colours have, 13 to 16 degrees from the NTSC hues of the pure colours). The luma is the old card's four simulated levels (0.416, 0.709, 1.160 and 1.460 V, reenigne) at the mean chroma 0, 0.5 or 1 of the wave, or the new card's weights 0.29 chroma + 0.10 R + 0.22 G + 0.07 B + 0.32 I. The chroma amplitude is the fundamental of the square wave over the black-to-white span: 0.4558 (old card) and 0.1846 (new card, about 2.5 times less saturated), or 0.2 for both when the television brings the burst to 40 IRE and the span to 100 IRE (an assumption on the sync level). The recognition is the nearest of the canonical RGBI colours (and PCem's dark yellow). The colours of the 640 x 200 mode (artifact colours), the 80-column text mode, the television's controls and the composite crosstalk are not modelled. **Shared code extended:** the `signal` kind's reference may be a list of RGB colours an emulator shows (`[[signal.reference.rgb_references]]`, each standing for a colour index) instead of a computed palette. Unit tests (the card's logic written out against the file, the waves' phases by their fundamentals, the recognition, the validation) and a GPU case against the oracle.
- **Sega Master System and Mark III colour DAC** (`source_colour_target = sega_master_system`, `source_colour_variant` 1 to 3: **1** the first VDP (315-5124, Mark III and SMS1) with TmEE's bench levels and the first step of blue raised (the default), **2** the first VDP as a capture card and scope read it, **3** the second VDP (315-5246) with TmEE's levels): a Master System picture with its 2-bit channels shown at the VDP's output levels in place of the plain 0, 85, 170, 255 (assumed input, not read in any emulator). Oracle tests, unit tests, GPU test.
- **SNK Neo Geo palette DAC** (`source_colour_target = snk_neo_geo`, `source_colour_variant` 1 to 4: **1** normal (the default), **2** the dark bit set on every colour, **3** the shadow on, **4** shadow and dark bit): a Neo Geo (MVS, AES, CD) picture with its 5-bit colour codes shown at the levels of the console's resistor-ladder palette DAC. Each channel is five outputs through 3900, 2200, 1000, 470 and 220 ohm (bits 0 to 4) into one node, so the 32 levels are the conductance of the set resistors over the total (0, 7.8, 13.8, 21.6, 30.4 ... 247.2, 255 of 255, weights 3.06, 5.42, 11.93, 25.38 and 54.22 percent, not binary), computed by the unit tests from the resistor values and kept rounded in the data file (provenance D: the resistors are those of MAME and Geolith, both BSD-3, read in their sources, no table copied). The dark bit (8200 ohm to ground) lowers every level by 0.985663, the shadow of the video output (150 ohm) by 0.557054 and both by 0.552576 (the pulldown only adds to the divider's total conductance, so the factor is the same for every code). **What the pixel cannot say:** an emulator picture carries neither the dark bit nor the shadow state, so the variants are states of the whole picture that the user chooses; MAME and Geolith render both in their own palettes (variants 3 and 4 would darken twice) and MAME's and Geolith's resistor palettes already hold the ladder levels (leave the target off; Geolith's 'resnet' palette also smooths neighbouring levels, its 'Raw' palette is 6 bits with the dark bit as the lowest bit and is read directly, half a code off in the darkest colours). The input is read as the plain 5-bit expansion (c x 8 + c / 4), recovered exactly, **not verified for any named emulator**. Shared code extended: a `curve` target may have variants, each a `scale` on the whole output (`[[curve.variants]]`; `crt_model::target::Curve::scale`, `ladder_output`), pushed as `out_scale`, no shader change. Oracle tests, unit tests, GPU test.
- **TMS9928A / TMS9929A colours** (`source_colour_target = texas_instruments_tms9928`, `source_colour_variant` 1 to 2: **1** the Texas Instruments data manual's levels (the default), **2** the oscilloscope readings of a TMS9929A in an SC-3000): a Sega SG-1000, SC-3000, MSX1, ColecoVision or TI-99/4A picture shown in the 15 colours the chip's luma and colour-difference outputs give, so the tube's own gamma and primaries take over. The levels are Y, R-Y and B-Y of Table 2-3 of the TMS9918A, TMS9928A, TMS9929A data manual (Texas Instruments, November 1982) and TmEE's scope readings of a TMS9929ANL (SMS Power forum, 2017, white 964 mV), which agree within 0.02 of white. The decode matrix is settled by the gamut: the differences at unit scale (R' = Y + (R-Y), B' = Y + (B-Y), G' from the BT.601 luma, clamped) keeps every channel inside the rails but the red of light red; reading them as BT.601 Pb and Pr puts five channels at 1.14 to 1.35 and is ruled out; the 'wrong yellows' of the first research pass could not be reproduced. Each pixel is recognised as the nearest of 30 reference entries (the 15 colours at the display exponents 1.1 and 1.5, since emulators show the chip at 1.0 to 1.6): the Wikipedia 'TMS9918' palette is recognised in full, a decode at any exponent from 1.0 to 1.6 loses at most one colour of 15 to a neighbour; **not checked against any emulator's own palette**. **Shared code extended:** the `signal` kind takes colour differences (`r_y`, `b_y`, `neutral`, per colour or per revision) and `[signal.reference] extra_gammas` (a reference set per exponent, the hardware colours repeated per set: no change to the shader or the colour table); a revision's `luma_mv` may be 0. The TMS9918A's own composite colours (luminance differing in cyan and dark green, chroma phases unknown), the machine encoders and the television are not modelled.
- **Atari 8-bit and 5200 colours from the GTIA signal** (`source_colour_target = atari_8bit_gtia`, `source_colour_variant` 1 to 6: **1** the 800XL (the default), **2** the 130XE, **3** the 800, **4** the 400, each at a hue delay of 25.5 degrees, **5** and **6** the 800XL with the delay at 23.5 and 27.5 degrees): an Atari 400, 800, XL, XE or 5200 picture shown in the colours an NTSC television decodes from the GTIA's signal, so the tube's own gamma and primaries take over. The 256 colours (16 hues of 16 luma levels, indexed like the colour register, hue x 16 + luma) are computed from the **measured luma** of 16 levels on four machines (Avery Lee, Altirra Hardware Reference Manual, Appendix D, Table 116: oscilloscope, +/- 8 mV; the 400 by a probe on R203), the manual's **saturation per machine** (Table 117: 23.9, 23.3, 35.0 and 30.6 percent of the luma range) and its hue formula (the phase of hue h is 180 - delay x (h - 1) degrees on the U axis, the burst at 180 degrees, minus U, -57 degrees behind I), through the composite model's decode matrix; a channel that goes negative is clamped, as on a tube. The same `signal` kind as the C64 and the NES, with each pixel recognised as the nearest of the 256 colours by comparison with a fixed reference palette that does not depend on the machine. The kind grows a **grid** (`[signal.grid]`: hues, lumas, the phase of hue 1, the colour index = hue x lumas + luma, one luma list for every hue) and a per-revision chroma `amplitude`, so a file does not list 256 colours. **Shared code extended:** a signal target now holds up to **256** colours (`crt_model::target::SIGNAL_MAX`, was 64) and the colour table in the frame uniform buffer is 512 `vec4` (8 KiB, was 2 KiB: the hardware colours at entries 0 up, the references from entry 256), the whole frame buffer 8288 bytes, within the 16384 bytes of `maxUniformBufferRange` that Vulkan guarantees; the other signal targets keep their outputs (GPU test unchanged: largest difference 2.4e-4 to 4.9e-4). **Estimated, not measured:** the chroma-to-burst ratio (the manual takes the chroma equal to the burst at 40 IRE after the television's gain control), the decode matrix, the television's tint, the hue delay of any real machine (25.5 degrees is the middle of the manual's 23.5 to 27.5), and the recognition reference, **which is not checked against any emulator's palette** (Altirra's and Atari800's are made by GPL code that is not read here): the classification is tested on computed palettes (the machines' own colours land within 0.1 of the intended colour for 248 to 256 of 256 colours, the saturated 800 for 169). Not modelled: PAL machines, the 5200's own board (same chip, no measurement), the pedestal of NTSC (the GTIA's black is 'blacker than black' on a television with the 7.5 IRE setup), artifact colours, the television's controls. Oracle `crt_model::target::Signal` (a test also recomputes the luma ladder, LUM0 36k to LUM3 4.7k ohm, which follows the measurement within 0.012 of the range), GPU test against it (largest difference 2.4e-4), `targets.rs` tests.

### Changed

- **The notes and provenance of the MSX2+ / turbo R (V9958) palette target now rest on the read Yamaha data books, no number changed.** The V9958's 5-bit DAC is documented linear (the booklet's block diagram labels it 'DAC (LINEAR)'; the linearity moves from an estimate to a sourced statement, the displayed levels are still derived), with the RGB output levels quoted (VRGB31 2.8 / 3.1 / 3.5 V, VRGB0 1.9 / 2.2 / 2.5 V, VP-P 0.8 / 0.9 / 1.1 V; the V9938's 3-bit DAC VP-P 0.65 / 0.8 / 1.00 V, no per-step table, not labelled linear: still an identity, no variant). The V9938's composite table has no colour-difference matrix or burst phase, so no colour composite model of the MSX2 can be built from the books (stated in the notes). Documentation only.

- **One white-scale policy for the 26 colour targets, no number changed.** A target never rescales white to hide or invent a level: its white is what its sourced model gives for the emulator's full white; a white a source states (the Game Boy Color's 240 of 255, the AGS-101's measured tinted white, the palettes' lightest shades) is kept, and where the sources leave the scale open (the Mega Drive's 255 against 238) the file says which it uses and why. Two tests: every file states its white, and the computed white of variant 1 of each target is pinned.

### Fixed

- **A colour target did nothing useful on a monochrome phosphor type.** `decode` took the BT.601 luma of the picture for any `phosphor_type` other than P22 before the `target` slot, so every colour-dependent target (the Amstrad CPC green monitor's 9 : 3 : 1 mix, the recognising C64, NES and Atari targets, the Game Boy palettes, the Virtual Boy) saw three equal channels: with P31 the CPC green screen was bit-identical to target off, and the C64 mapped red, blue, orange, brown and dark grey to one grey. `decode` now keeps the colour while `source_colour_target` is not `none`, and the `target` slot takes the BT.601 luma of its own output on a monochrome phosphor, so the target acts on the colour and the later stages still get three equal channels. With no target the picture is bit for bit what it was; the SNES black boost and Neo Geo curves now act on the colour and then the luma instead of on the grey (a few codes on a monochrome phosphor). The notes of the CPC green screen, the description of `source_colour_target` and the reference say so.
- The **Colour variant** combo showed the last variant when `source_colour_variant` was past the last variant of the chosen target (a saved number 9 after switching to a target with four), while the GPU stage uses the first. It now shows the first (`Target::variant_index`).

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-20

ultimateCRT v0.3.0 is the second public release (MIT licence, binaries not code-signed). Since v0.2.0 the captured picture reaches the GPU without a trip through the CPU: zero-copy capture is the default, with an automatic fallback to the CPU copy and a Capture choice in the menu's Source section. The phosphor persistence trail was repaired (it could vanish when frames arrive unevenly) and has new afterglow controls; the Optics panel gains glow (bloom) and vignette controls and no longer lifts the blacks by itself, and the Geometry panel gains chromatic aberration controls. The output can be made click-through (`Ctrl+Alt+F6`), the window title and the menu show notices for a minimised source and for a move to a monitor driven by another GPU, and the framed window snaps to a screen edge. A three-model code review round and a live review fixed crash and robustness problems, notably an intermittent crash of zero-copy capture at attach, a persistence trail lost with uneven frame pacing, and a white flash when the window opens.

Known limits: the last fixes of this release (the zero-copy attach crash, the persistence trail, the window that now appears after its first frame) are covered by automated tests and by 45 consecutive zero-copy starts without a crash, but this exact build has not yet been re-checked by hand on an unlocked desktop (the picker without arguments, the white flash at start, the live persistence trail and the other interactive checks are still to be confirmed); zero-copy capture is not yet validated with a wide range of games and drivers (the Capture choice in the Source section goes back to the CPU copy); with click-through on, the framed window cannot be moved with the mouse; SDR output only; window capture only (no exclusive-fullscreen games); developed and tested on one NVIDIA laptop (AMD and Intel GPUs and other PCs are untested beyond it).

The entries below are the detail, grouped by Added, Changed, Fixed and Security.

### Added

- Zero-copy capture (`[global.capture] path`, `--capture-path auto|zero-copy|cpu` for one run; ARCHITECTURE A29). The captured client area is copied on the GPU into one of three textures shared between Direct3D 11 and Vulkan and read in place by the first stage of the chain, without the CPU read-back and upload: measured headless on the reference PC, the capture thread's copy drops from about 3.1 to 3.8 ms to 0.2 to 0.3 ms per frame at 2560 x 1440 (6.4 to 7.4 ms to 0.4 to 0.5 ms at 4K) and the upload goes (QUESTIONS CAP-2). The picture is bit-identical to the CPU copy. Zero-copy needs capture and rendering on one adapter and a driver that imports the textures. The automatic detections still read the picture back, about ten times a second. It is not yet validated with a wide range of games and drivers.

It runs on the stage's image pyramid (each lobe on the level that matches its size); the `low` quality tier uses coarser levels. The README recipe: a gentle glow of weight 0.12, radius 6 mm, threshold 0.6 and falloff 0 keeps the blacks black; the wide lobes of a falloff above 0 lift them by a few codes.

### Fixed

- Zero-copy capture (the default) crashed intermittently at attach, and once at a source resize (live review 2026-11-20: 10 crashes in 35 starts on the RTX, 4 in 5 on the Intel UHD, none in 12 with the CPU copy), with `range start index ... out of range for slice of length 0` in the integer-scale detection. Cause: while a detection runs every frame is read back for the CPU analyses, and when the main loop had not yet taken a frame's sample, the capture thread took that buffer to refill it for the next frame; the main loop could then take the earlier frame, marked as carrying its own pixels, with an empty buffer. The capture thread now leaves a sample that was not taken in place, and each frame records the size of the picture its buffer holds. The CPU analyses (black-frame check, Auto picture area, integer-scale detection, smoothed-source warning, the CPU upload and `--save-capture`) read pixels only through a checked sample (`capture::Sample`, new module `analysis`), and every detector refuses a buffer that does not hold its region instead of indexing it. Reproduced and covered headless in `tests/zero_copy.rs`; a fuzz test feeds the detectors random buffer lengths and sizes (QUESTIONS CAP-2).
- Phosphor persistence showed no trail at all in the window under uneven frame pacing, as after the A/B bypass in the live test of 2026-11-20 (the trail stayed 0 for 10 s until a restart). The window loop's measured frame periods alternate between about 1 ms and about 33 ms at 60 Hz when frames in flight let a frame be recorded right after the previous one; the automatic trail rate, a trimmed mean of 31 periods, then jumped by about 13 % with each new period and refitted the decay, which restarts the history primed to the picture, at nearly every frame (599 of 600 frames in the stage test): the picture was the one without persistence. The automatic rate is now the mean of the last 64 periods (a stall is capped at 2.5 times the mean), a new rate is taken only once it has settled, and each frame decays the trail by the mean period (a gap longer than three periods by its own length), so uneven intervals neither restart nor shorten the trail and it no longer flickers with the pacing. Tests: stage unit tests (alternating and phase-locked random intervals: at most one restart in 10 s, the step within 1.6 % of the period), `tests/persistence_chain.rs` (the whole chain with uneven pacing, the A/B bypass, and persistence off then on: the trail ratios equal the 60 Hz oracle within 1 %; 0 with the previous estimate), and `tests/stage_state_resume.rs` (the other stages that keep state across frames resume after the bypass and after their switch). A live moving-bar trail that looked shorter than the headless one is a measurement effect, not a defect (`live_bar_measurement_explained`); the red trail of 16 seen live is the SMPTE-C tube primaries of the preset seen in the panel's sRGB (0 with sRGB primaries). README "Phosphor persistence" gives both sets of numbers.
- A white area showed for about 0.3 s when the output window opened: the window is now shown once its first frame is presented (or after one second without one), and its client area is black until then.
- Zero-copy: after a capture restart, a reattach or a new attach, the renderer could keep sampling the previous session's shared textures (each session numbered its texture pools from 1); pool generations are now unique in the process and the renderer re-imports on any new pool.
- A DPI change queued in the same moment as the menu closing was dropped; a title-bar drag across monitors of different scaling now keeps the size Windows suggests.
- A wrong-typed `first_run_done` or `click_through` in `settings.toml` no longer makes the whole file unusable.

### Security

- Preset files are read at most 256 KB whatever size they report (import, and the presets folder at start): a larger file is skipped with a warning, the bound holds for a file that grows while it is read, and deep nesting and huge names give messages instead of failures.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-20

ultimateCRT v0.2.0 is the first public release (MIT licence, binaries not code-signed). It captures the window of a game or a program, runs it through a virtual CRT tube on the GPU and shows the result in a second window that can go fullscreen over the source without taking its keyboard focus; nothing is injected into the captured program. What is in it:

- A window picker (search, icons, preview), automatic detection of the integer upscale and of the picture inside black bars, per-game profiles, and reattachment when the captured window closes and comes back.
- The physical chain in linear light on the GPU (Vulkan, FP16): source decode, video amplifier, gun law and white balance, automatic beam limiting, scanline beam with spot growth, geometry and convergence, aperture grille, slot mask or delta dot mask, phosphor saturation and persistence, faceplate optics (halation, glare), SDR output transform; ten tube presets from published data; optional composite video, interlace, ageing and burn-in (all off by default).
- A settings menu inside the output window with every parameter live, preset management (save, rename, duplicate, import, export), rebindable hotkeys, an interface size setting, a quality tier (exact, standard, fast, low) with an optional automatic drop, and an About section.
- `pixel-demo.exe`, a demo and test source, and an offline `render` command.

The executables are not code-signed: Windows SmartScreen may warn on first run. Check the zip against the SHA-256 given in these notes (the README and `RUNNING.txt` say how). The bare F10 and F11 keys are global hotkeys while the program runs (both can be rebound).

Known limits: SDR output only; window capture only, so no exclusive-fullscreen games; developed and tested on one NVIDIA laptop (AMD and Intel GPUs and other PCs are untested beyond it) and checked live mostly with the bundled demo source and a few windows; several live checks are listed below as "not checked live". Frames go from the GPU to the CPU and back (a zero-copy path is a later step).

- Video amplifier (`amp` slot, `shaders/amp.comp`, `params/amp.toml`): Gaussian low-pass of the drive voltage before the gun law (`amp_bandwidth_mhz`, `amp_pixel_clock_mhz`), asymmetric rise/fall (`amp_fall_rise_ratio`), optional peaking (`amp_peaking_gain`, `amp_peaking_mhz`), K sub-samples per native pixel each holding the exact box mean of the amplifier output, clamped to the local input range (slew limiter); the defaults are an exact zero-order hold. Oracle `crt_model::beam::gaussian_step_box_mean`.
- Gun law and white balance (`gun` slot, `shaders/gun.comp`, `params/gun.toml`): `gun_law` power (default, 2.4), gog (contrast, brightness, per-gun cutoff and drive), bt1886 (Lw, Lb) and bt1886_annex1; optional white retune relative to the tube white, to the target `gun_retune_white_xy` (`gun_white_balance`, default off; gains (1, 1, 1) when the target is the tube white); the `srgb` source mode inverts the nominal curve of the active law and `gun_gamma` is clamped to >= 0.1; a disabled `gun` slot shows V as linear light, which is lighter than the gun-on picture. Oracle `crt_model::gun`.
- Automatic beam limiting and raster breathing (`abl` slot after `gun`, `shaders/abl.comp` and `shaders/reduce.comp`, `params/abl.toml`, T-35): the frame's mean beam current (GPU reduction, FP32) is low-passed on real frame times (`abl_tau_s`, default 0.1667 s) and lowers the beam current of the next frame above `abl_threshold_ma` (1.4 mA) with g = 1 / (1 + k max(0, I - I_th)), k set by `abl_full_white_ratio` (0.5); raster breathing `raster_scale` = 1 + `abl_breathing` (APL - `abl_breathing_ref`), used by the geometry stage (T-37); `abl_probe` diagnostic. Off by default (exact identity). Oracle `crt_model::abl`.
- Beam, vertical scanline profile (T-36a): each native row is drawn as a scanline whose Gaussian spot (plus an optional wide tail) is integrated over every host pixel, with the flat-field brightness independent of the spot width. Parameters `beam_fwhm_mid_mm` (default 0.45 mm), `beam_tail_weight`, `beam_tail_sigma_ratio`. Active once the tube is placed (px per mm and line pitch set); until then the screen stage keeps the nearest-sample copy.
- Beam, horizontal spot and spot growth (T-36b): along each scanline the amp samples are blurred by the same round spot and integrated over every host pixel (energy per source pixel conserved, flat fields unchanged), and the spot widens with each gun's beam current, FWHM^2 = d0^2 + k I^(2n). Parameters `beam_fwhm_peak_mm` (default 0.8 mm, PVM-20M4U) and `beam_growth_exponent` (default 0.5); `beam_fwhm_mid_mm` is the width at mid drive (beam current 0.5^2.4). Per-channel convergence hook in `beam.glsl`.
- Beam quality (T-36c): `beam_quality` `standard` (default, separable per workgroup tile, within 0.5 % of white mean of the exact form on the test patterns) or `reference` (exact 2D sum of every sample's own spot).
- Mask (T-39a, T-39b, T-39c): every host pixel takes the exact area average of the R, G and B phosphor elements it covers (pitch, fill and edge softness in physical units), normalised so that flat white keeps its brightness. `mask_type` selects the aperture grille (with optional damper-wire shadows), the slot mask (`mask_slot_period`, `mask_slot_bridge`) or the delta dot mask (`mask_dot_fill`). Below 3 host pixels per triad (and the matching limits vertically) the mask is averaged out by default (no host-grid moire), with a blend band `mask_tier_blend` and `mask_tier` to force either behaviour. Parameters `mask_enabled`, `mask_pitch_mm` (default 0.31 mm), `mask_stripe_fill`, `mask_edge_softness`, `mask_damper_count` (default 0), `mask_damper_width_mm`, `mask_damper_edge_mm`.
- Phosphor saturation (T-40, `phosphor_saturation`, off by default): L = I / (1 + s I) per channel before the persistence history.
- Phosphor persistence (`phosphor` slot, `shaders/phosphor.comp`, `params/phosphor.toml`, T-41): optional frame-to-frame afterglow from the measured P22 decay (Kuhn 2002): a bright frame leaves a green and blue trail of about 5.4 / 4.7 % of its light one frame later at 60 Hz, then about 3 % and 2 %, red none; a static picture keeps its brightness. Parameters `phosphor_persistence` (default off: the picture passes unchanged), `phosphor_frame_rate_hz` (60, the output refresh rate) and `phosphor_tail_strength_rgb` (0.07 to 1). The afterglow is cleared on a source size change and on a new capture session. Oracle `crt_model::phosphor::DecayFit::frame_feedback`.
- Geometry and convergence (T-37, `shaders/include/geometry.glsl`, `params/geometry.toml`): optional curved faceplate (`geometry_curvature` flat, spherical or cylindrical, `geometry_radius_mm`, `geometry_eye_distance_mm`; the raster lies on the surface uniform in arc length), residual pincushion/barrel, trapezoid and rotation, overscan per axis, and per-channel misconvergence (static dx, dy in mm plus a radial term growing toward the corners). ABL raster breathing (`abl_breathing`) now resizes the picture about the tube centre. The mask stays fixed on the tube. Every default is the identity: the picture is unchanged. The tube image carries a margin around the raster (`raster_margin`, 4 sigma of the spot at twice the peak current plus the largest misconvergence, limited by the room in the output) where the spot's skirt is drawn instead of cut; the live output adds it whenever the `screen` slot runs (a bypassed `screen` has no skirt and no margin). The `standard` beam quality keeps its separable fast path for the margin, overscan, `raster_scale` and misconvergence; a curved faceplate, pincushion, trapezoid or rotation use the two-pass fast path of T-91 (see Changed).
- Visible opening (T-37 review): the light of `screen` is clipped to the tube's visible area (`tube_visible_height_mm` x `tube_aspect`) with rounded corners, `geometry_opening_corner_radius_mm` (default 0, square; CRT.md 15 to 40 mm on older tubes): exactly 0 outside, antialiased over one host pixel along a curved outline. Not implemented and deferred (QUESTIONS PHY-11): S-linearity residuals (CRT.md has no residual profile) and refraction through the faceplate (no outer radius or glass thickness per curved tube).
- Faceplate optics (`optics` slot, `shaders/optics*.comp`, `params/optics.toml`, oracle `crt_model::optics`, T-44 and T-45; CRT.md rule 7, QUESTIONS PHY-10), off by default (`optics_enable`, D-6, D-24: the slot is skipped as an exact identity): phosphor-layer diffusion (`optics_diffusion_weight`, `optics_diffusion_mm`), the halation ring of the faceplate glass with its shape derived from the glass (`optics_glass_thickness_mm`, `optics_glass_index`, `optics_glass_transmission`: edge at 1.747 times the thickness, Fresnel haze inside, absorbed tail) and its amplitude as the fraction of light in the halo (`optics_halation_weight`, 1.5 %), veiling glare proportional to the mean picture level (`optics_glare_weight`), vignette (`optics_vignette_corner`, corner / centre), glass tint (`optics_tint_rgb`), diffuse room-light reflection (`optics_ambient_lux`, `optics_screen_reflectance`, `optics_white_nits`); all redistribute light, so a white field keeps its level. Halo method chosen by a headless cost study (`optics_halo_method`, `optics_halo_taps`): the exact kernel on one pyramid level (error 0.010 % mean, 0.13 % max of white; about 1.1 ms for the whole slot at 1920 x 1080 with every effect on, on a clock-limited GPU); a bloom-like pyramid mix and full-resolution Gaussians stay selectable for comparison. Optics panel in the settings menu (generated from the table). Presets 01 to 09 carry their faceplate thickness, halation weight and (05) transmission, and keep the stage off. Tests: `crt-model` oracle unit tests (ring position, kernel normalisation, Fresnel, FFT convolution, energy, vignette, glare, ambient), stage unit tests, `tests/stage_optics.rs` (identity bit exact, point-source ring and energy, edge, flat white and game-like frames against the oracle, diffusion energy, vignette ratio, glare linear in APL, tint and ambient, FP16 round to nearest) and the ignored cost study.
- Tube ageing (T-42, QUESTIONS PHY-14, `params/ageing.toml`, opt-in, `ageing_enable` off by default: the chain is unchanged bit for bit). Phosphor efficiency loss per colour from the hours of use (`ageing_hours`, `ageing_mean_level`, `ageing_white_cd_m2`), Pfahnl law 1 / (1 + c Q) with Q the charge in reference hours of full white at 350 cd/m2, calibrated on the one sourced point (15 % luminance loss after 20,000 h at 350 cd/m2) with relative colour rates (`ageing_rates_rgb`, default blue twice green, red half: white drifts warm); cathode wear per gun in `gun` (`ageing_cathode_loss_rgb`, `ageing_cutoff_drift_rgb`); spot growth of a worn gun (`ageing_spot_growth`, both beam widths scaled on the CPU so the skirt margin, the warp plan and the headroom readout follow). New chain slot `wear` between `screen` and `phosphor` (`shaders/wear.comp`), skipped as an identity while ageing and burn-in are off. Oracle `crt_model::ageing`.
- Burn-in (T-43, QUESTIONS PHY-14, SCP-6, `params/burnin.toml`, opt-in, `burnin_enable` off by default). This deliberately degrades the picture: static bright parts (a HUD, a letterbox edge, a logo) leave a ghost tinted by the colour that wore. A per-colour wear map of 640 x 480 texels over the tube's visible area (independent of the host resolution) accumulates the beam charge every frame (`shaders/wear_accumulate.comp`, exact area mean per texel, FP32 with compensated summation so real-time increments are not lost), at `burnin_acceleration` tube hours per real hour (1 = real time, 0 typed freezes the map), and lowers the efficiency with the same law as ageing. Saved per tube preset in `%APPDATA%\ultimateCRT\tubes\<preset>.state` (magic, schema version, size, CRC-32, atomic write; a missing, corrupt or other-version file is a new tube with a warning and a `.bak` copy; another size is resampled), every 5 minutes from an asynchronous GPU copy written on a background thread, when the preset changes, and at exit. Menu buttons "Burn in N h now" (`burnin_now_hours`) and "New tube (reset wear)" in the new "Ageing and burn-in" panel; `--reset-burnin` starts a new tube. Oracle `crt_model::burnin`; controller and file format `src/tube_state.rs`. Tests: oracle unit tests (decay curve, calibration, accumulation, per-colour independence, ghost only inside the worn area, resampling), file and controller unit tests, and `tests/stage_ageing.rs` (one GPU program: identity when off, blue x 0.85 at 15 % loss, cathode wear, spot growth 1.2, map and ghost against the oracle, calibration point, real-time increments kept, map frozen at acceleration 0 over 1000 frames, asynchronous snapshot, save and load bit exact, corrupt file).
- Interlace, manual field mode (T-32, QUESTIONS SRC-7, `params/interlace.toml`, oracle `crt_model::interlace`, tests `tests/stage_interlace.rs`; optional, `interlace_mode` off by default and bit-identical when off). A capture carries no field parity, so the user declares it: `alternate_frames_are_fields` (each captured frame is one field, the bottom field lands half a line pitch lower) or `split_woven_frame` (a woven frame: each refresh draws only the even or only the odd rows, at twice the line spacing, at their own positions). `interlace_field_order` (top or bottom field first) and `interlace_field_select` (alternate every output refresh, or lock the top or bottom field for inspection; a locked field of a woven frame is the 240p double-strike look). The field changes the beam raster only (`beam_raster`, all beam paths); the decay between fields comes from phosphor persistence. The field code is compiled behind a specialization constant, so the default pipelines are the progressive ones. The live title and console show a notice when the output refresh does not match the field rate. Panel: Beam and scanlines.
- Output transform (`encode` stage): tube primaries and white to sRGB colour matrix (`output_match_srgb`), soft shoulder (`output_knee`), clip counters (`Renderer::clip_counts`), and a final 1 LSB dither (`output_dither`: off, ordered, noise). `crt-model::output` is the f64 oracle; `crt-model` is now a regular dependency of `ultimatecrt`.

#### Quality tiers
- Quality tiers (T-48, QUESTIONS PRF-2; `params/quality.toml`, `src/quality.rs`, menu panel Performance): `quality_tier` = `exact` (the evaluation as before the tiers, bit for bit), `standard` (default: the beam of an unscaled flat tube on the line-image path, within one FP16 step of exact, `screen` 2.4 to 3 times cheaper on flat tubes), `fast` (also amplifier sub-samples capped to the host pixels per source pixel, at least 4, and the line-image path on scaled flat rasters; within 0.82 % of white mean, 2.7 % max), `low` (also a beam reach of 3 sigma, specialization constant 1 of `beam.glsl`, and a coarser warp grid; within 0.87 % mean, 2.7 % max). A tier changes cost parameters only, never a look parameter; a preset change keeps it. Measured cost and error per tier: ARCHITECTURE 3.7 "Quality tiers".
- Automatic quality drop on sustained overrun (QUESTIONS OUT-6; `quality::AutoQuality`, pure and unit tested): with `quality_auto` (off by default) the tier in force drops one step after 3 s of GPU frame time above the refresh period of the output's monitor (`output::refresh_hz`), and comes back one step after 10 s below 65 % of it (the wait doubles, up to 160 s, after a relapse within 30 s); the parameters are not changed (`Renderer::set_quality_drop`), the console, the window title and the overlay (row "Quality") show the tier in force.

### Changed
- New default hotkeys that do not fire on AltGr (review D-01): quit `Ctrl+Alt+End` (was `Ctrl+Alt+Q`), overlay `Ctrl+Alt+F9` (was `Ctrl+Alt+O`), A/B bypass `Ctrl+Alt+F7` (was `Ctrl+Alt+B`); F10, F11 and `Ctrl+Alt+PageDown`/`PageUp` are unchanged. Reason: Windows sends AltGr as LeftCtrl+RightAlt, so a `Ctrl+Alt+<letter>` hotkey also fired on AltGr+letter and swallowed that character in every program while the app ran (`@` is AltGr+Q on a German keyboard, so typing an e-mail address quit the app; `\` on Czech, Slovak and Hungarian, `ó` AltGr+O on Polish). The new chords use function and navigation keys only, which type nothing under AltGr on any layout, and avoid the Intel graphics hotkeys (Ctrl+Alt+F1, F3, F4, F8, F11, F12) and the arrows (screen rotation). Fallbacks: `Ctrl+Alt+Shift+End`, `Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F9`, `Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F7`. Quit is now rebindable like the others (Change and Default in the menu, `quit` in `[global.hotkeys]`), and it is never left unbound: a chord the system refuses keeps the old one, and when even that fails its default and its fallback are tried; Reset all brings it back too. Choosing a `Ctrl+Alt+<letter, digit or Space>` chord in the menu checks the installed keyboard layouts (`ToUnicodeEx` with Ctrl and Alt down, new `keyboard_layout.rs`) and warns, naming the character and the layout, when AltGr types something on that key. Settings files: `[global.hotkeys]` now holds only the chords that differ from their default (`hotkey_defaults = 2` in `[global]`); a file of an earlier build, which wrote every chord, has the old defaults it still holds upgraded at the first start (a chord the user changed stays, and so does an old default whose new default another action already uses), is written back at once, and a note says what changed (window list, Hotkeys rows, console). README, ENV, ARCHITECTURE, DESIGN, `--help`, the first-run hint, `RUNNING.txt`, `scripts/check-hotkeys.ps1` and `check-menu.ps1` use the new chords.
- The GPU chosen in the menu is remembered by a stable identity (review D-02): `[global.gpu] adapter` holds the adapter name with its PCI vendor, device and subsystem ids and the LUID only as a hint (`NVIDIA RTX A2000 Laptop GPU (PCI 10DE:25B8:0A3D1028, LUID 00000000:00017801)`; `--list-adapters` prints the value of each adapter). Reason: Windows keeps an adapter LUID only until the system restarts, so after a reboot the stored LUID could match nothing and the app silently ran the automatic choice, possibly on the integrated GPU, with the warning only on a console a release build does not have. The adapter is matched at start by vendor and device id (and subsystem id when both are known), the same name preferred (a driver update that renames it still matches); two identical GPUs are told apart by the LUID hint within one boot, else by their rank in the DXGI order (`#2`). Older values keep working: a name part as before, and a bare LUID, which is replaced by the adapter's identity while it still names one. A configured adapter that is missing (or `--gpu` with an unknown index) is now shown in the window title, the window list and the menu's GPU panel (which names a remembered adapter that is gone "(not found)"), not only in a `warning:` console line.
- ABL (T-48): in every quality tier but `exact` the frame APL is summed in parallel (`reduce_partial.comp`, `reduce_final.comp`) instead of by one workgroup (about 0.35 ms at K = 16 on a 1080p tube); the sum differs by FP32 rounding only.
- Lost source (A23): after the wait (`[global.source] wait_s`, the menu, or `--wait-source`; now also for a framed output) the source is given up and the window picker comes back with a message, instead of a frozen frame; a fullscreen output becomes the non-activating framed window of F11 rather than an activating dropped window. `--window` no longer defaults to `pixel-demo` (without it the picker opens); `--save-capture` needs `--window`, `--pattern` or `--image`. The crop and divisor modes of the Source and placement panel apply live. The window title names the attached program. `LostAction::GiveUp`, `LostWatch::set_wait`.
- Impact analysis by items (`scripts/impact.ps1`, `ENV.md` Test policy): a changed `ultimatecrt` module no longer pulls in every test that depends on it by module. The script reads `git diff -U0`, outlines the old and the new file, finds the items whose lines changed (comment-only and blank-line changes count for nothing), follows a private change up the call chain to the public items of the file (child modules also see the private items), and selects only the tests and modules that mention those items; OS glue (`output`, `hotkeys`, `capture`, `cli`) is a leaf unless a public signature, constant, type or re-export changed; tests that run the binary are selected for a glue change or when the entry module `app` is reached. A module that defines, qualifies (`menu::on_activate`) or imports from another crate (`Options`) a name is not counted as a user of the changed module's item. Unknown cases (a changed line outside every item, a changed impl header, a glob re-export, an unbalanced outline, a new file, `-Files`) keep the module-level rules, and the FULL fallbacks are unchanged. The three merges of the window-procedure work selected 28 of 30 test programs, they now select 4; `-Validate 15` averages 13.3 selected programs per commit instead of 15.0 with no miss. 11 new self-test cases on a git fixture (private fn, pub body, pub signature, pub const, test-only, comment-only, flag parsing, usage text, non-glue private fn, private fn seen by a child module, pixel-demo only).
- SDR headroom as settings (T-84, audit B1/M1, owner decision D-16; `src/headroom.rs`, `shaders/encode.frag`, `shaders/include/output.glsl`, `shaders/include/mask.glsl`): a full-white field has elements above panel white (resolved mask stripes about 1 / fill, scanline peaks 1.4 to 2.3 times the mean), which the T-46 hard clip cut by 12 to 54 % of the white mean on the presets at 2560 x 1440. New `output_headroom_mode` (default `auto`, see the next entry): `shoulder` (`output_knee` now 0.6, roll-off reaching panel white exactly at `output_shoulder_white`, 0 = automatic from the headroom readout; no roll-off when the readout is at most 1, so peak-free chains stay exact), `physical` (exposure divided by the readout x (1 + `output_headroom_margin`), nothing clips), `mask_depth` (new `mask_depth` limits the mask contrast, mean kept, NON-PHYSICAL; exposure then derived) and `clip` (the previous behaviour). The CPU headroom readout (mask peak x scanline peak x white matrix maximum x exposure; `crt_model::output::Headroom`, `Mask::peak_value`, `beam::scanline_pixel_peak`, `output::shoulder_rate`) is shown through `Renderer::headroom`. The exposure provenance and notes no longer claim 'no clip' and give the readout and the measured white kept per mode. Measured: the default shoulder keeps 1 to 4 points LESS of the white mean than the hard clip (it removes clip bands and tints), physical keeps the mean exact at 25 to 69 % of the intended white. The `encode` push constants carry the resolved exposure, knee and rate (72 bytes).
